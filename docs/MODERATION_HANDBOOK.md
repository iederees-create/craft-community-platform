# Tuftlings Moderation and Incident Handbook

Status: Draft operational policy  
Audience: administrators, moderators, pattern maintainers, and support staff

## Severity levels

### P0 — Immediate danger

Examples: credible threat of violence, child sexual exploitation material, active doxxing with imminent risk, credible self-harm emergency.

Actions:

1. Restrict visibility immediately.
2. Preserve the minimum necessary evidence in an administrator-only record.
3. Prevent further contact where technically possible.
4. Escalate to the platform owner.
5. Contact appropriate emergency or legal channels when required.
6. Do not confront a suspected offender in a way that increases danger.

### P1 — Severe abuse or exploitation

Examples: targeted harassment, hate content, sexual harassment, non-consensual intimate content, repeated block evasion, malicious piracy, entitlement fraud.

Target response: initial action within 24 hours.

Possible actions: removal, feature restriction, temporary suspension, permanent suspension after human review.

### P2 — Community or intellectual-property violation

Examples: uncredited remix, copied pattern, spam campaign, deceptive promotion, misleading test status, repeated hostile behaviour.

Target response: within 72 hours.

### P3 — Quality or etiquette concern

Examples: off-topic posting, low-context promotion, insufficient attribution that appears accidental, unhelpful but non-abusive replies.

Target response: within five working days.

## Standard case record

Every moderation case should record:

- Case identifier
- Reporter and subject identifiers
- Content identifiers
- Rule category and severity
- Evidence location
- Immediate containment action
- Moderator reasoning
- Decision and duration
- Member notification status
- Appeal deadline and outcome
- Related cases
- Final reviewer
- Timestamps

Do not place sensitive evidence in public comments or general analytics.

## Decision workflow

1. Triage: confirm the report concerns Tuftlings and classify severity.
2. Contain: restrict harmful material or interactions without deleting evidence prematurely.
3. Investigate: review the reported item, surrounding context, account history, blocks, and applicable permission.
4. Decide: choose the narrowest effective intervention.
5. Explain: identify the rule, action, duration, and appeal route.
6. Record: write an audit entry without unnecessary personal information.
7. Follow up: check for retaliation, evasion, or recurring product defects.

## Intellectual-property workflow

- A normal community report is not automatically a legal takedown notice.
- Preserve the disputed content and attribution chain privately.
- Restrict access if continued availability creates clear harm.
- Give the uploader an opportunity to provide authorship or permission evidence when appropriate.
- Route formal legal notices to the designated owner contact.
- Never decide trademark or copyright ownership from visual similarity alone.
- Paid Tuftlings pattern files must never be posted to public project media.

## Pattern-safety escalation

Reports about stitch counts, structural failure, choking hazards, unsafe eyes, loose accessories, needle hazards, flammability, or misleading child-safety language must also create a Pattern Lab erratum case.

A safety report may require:

- Pausing downloads
- Moving a pattern version from published or approved back to testing
- Notifying entitled buyers
- Publishing corrected instructions
- Recording affected versions
- Preserving the original release for audit
- Requiring new physical testing

Moderators cannot mark a pattern physically tested based only on photographs or AI review.

## Sanction guidance

Use history and context; do not apply a mechanical strike system.

- Guidance: first-time, low-risk mistake.
- Warning: clear violation with limited harm.
- Content restriction: uncertainty, investigation, age/privacy concern, or temporary containment.
- Temporary feature limit: repeated spam, hostile posting, or misuse of uploads.
- Temporary suspension: serious or repeated violation.
- Permanent suspension: severe exploitation, credible danger, repeated evasion, or persistent serious abuse after human review.

## Appeals

Eligible members should receive:

- The rule cited
- The content or behaviour category
- The action and duration
- An appeal deadline
- A way to provide context or evidence

An appeal should be reviewed by someone other than the original decision-maker where staffing allows. The result and reasoning must be recorded.

## Blocking

Blocking must prevent the blocked account from:

- Commenting on the blocker's projects
- Mentioning or directly notifying the blocker
- Joining restricted spaces owned by the blocker
- Viewing non-public content belonging to the blocker

Public project visibility may still expose content unless the product explicitly hides it. The UI must explain this honestly.

## Moderator safety

- Use role accounts with least privilege.
- Never download suspicious files onto a personal device.
- Avoid exposing moderator identities unnecessarily.
- Rotate reviewers for distressing material.
- Record conflicts of interest and reassign the case.
- Never use moderation access for personal disputes.

## Transparency reporting

Prepare aggregate reporting without identifying members:

- Report counts by category
- Median response times
- Actions taken
- Appeals and reversals
- Pattern-safety cases
- Repeat abuse rates
- Systemic product changes

Do not publish tiny categories that could re-identify a member.
